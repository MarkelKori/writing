Starfall

Markel Kori / 17 December 2022 / 12 min read

The city lights were fading. The crystal at its centre was using the last of its strength to illuminate the square around it… Sometimes I noticed its faint violet glow vanish for a fraction of a second. It was dying, like the whole world, but there was still time before the end.

My Wings touched down beside the service entrance on the hundred-and-thirtieth floor. Above that there was nothing but grey cloud, swiftly disappearing as though trying to get away before nightfall. The Wing folded itself away, hovering in the air to wait for me. Without taking off my helmet, I jumped onto the ledge, fastened a hook to it, and began lowering myself past the windows like a climber. Before long an orange-lit room brought me to a halt. I tapped on the glass, looking at the girl inside.

Her heart must have stopped… And she was covered in tears. A moment ago she had been sitting on the sofa, sobbing. Now she was running to me, lifting the window, holding out her hands… I stepped inside, the window closed, the lights dimmed. We embraced, and my helmet disappeared.

“Charlie, Charlie,” she kept saying. “It’s you… You’re alive!”

“Of course I’m alive, darling,” I replied. “Where else would I be? Why were you crying?”

She raised eyes filled with oceans of tears.

“The crystals are dying. I thought you’d died too… All these nights I’ve been alone, listening to explosions, gunfire… It never stopped for more than a few minutes. What’s going to happen to us?”

“If only I knew, Clara. Even if these are the last days we have left to live, isn’t that all the more reason to enjoy everything we’ve got? God, I’m so glad to see you again!”

I held her once more and kissed her several times. But we had to spare the time.

“I can’t stay long. I only came to say hello… I hope you haven’t finished my brandy?”

She moved her nose from side to side, meaning no, and wiped the tears from her cheeks. Their salty taste lingered on my lips a little longer, until I washed it away with brandy. I took a few little bonotos from the basket to carry with me; they were very good at keeping hunger at bay.

“How long can the city hold out? How long will we have light?” Clara asked, wringing her hands.

“Two weeks, maybe. If the people at the top weren’t busy stealing crystals—if they’d spare a thought for ordinary people, even at the end of the world—we’d have enough for a month or more.”

“What… What do you think we can do?”

I shrugged.

“There’s nothing to be done. That’s our nature. We Nebidenians depend on these glowing minerals for our lives. The only thing I want before I die is to stop being the coward I’ve always been. I want revenge on that madman Lamens for starting the war. What a bastard!” I struck my glass against the table so loudly I almost broke it. Then I headed back towards the window.

“Stay, Charlie.” Clara caught my hand.

“If I stay, they’ll find me soon and kill us both. Don’t you understand?!”

She closed her eyes and drew a deep breath.

“All right. A little longer… Just don’t cry.” I held her, and we stood like that for a couple of minutes. Perhaps she had needed it. Perhaps I had needed it…

That day I never returned to the Wing, never climbed to the top floor to disappear into the sky again… I had planned to kill Lamens with a homemade weapon powered by small crystals of midenium—the same substance as the crystal now feeding the city. Over the past few days I had discovered his whereabouts, and someone had given me a plan of his underground fortress in exchange for crystals. Having once designed buildings, stations and fortifications of all kinds, I had spotted the weak points at once. If only they had let me fly away that evening, I would have found the bastard and…

But there was a knock at the door. Government officials. As it turned out, they did not even want to kill me… We came to an agreement, and I opened up. Clara hid, then came out again because everything was all right. They offered me amnesty in exchange for my cooperation.

It was General Nikopo. He spoke to me in person while I, still suspecting a trap, kept my weapon trained on him.

“There’s very little time or energy left,” he said. “As of today, we’ve abolished the death penalty for desertion. Every case will be considered individually, but we want to pardon you. Because we truly need you.” He took a step closer, and I felt all the more keenly how much taller and larger every other Nebidenian was than me. But I was the one holding the gun.

“Stay where you are!” I ordered. “You behaved like savages. Executing people! I hate you. How do you imagine I could help you?”

“Not us. You’ll be helping all Nebidenians, if you agree. We’ve been developing this project for the past three weeks, ever since we realised the crystals were dwindling and their numbers would never recover. In part, it was designed… Specifically for you.”

“For me?”

“Yes. If we budget enough to survive another ten days, there’ll be a small amount of energy left to put the plan into action. But it will only be enough if you’re the one who carries it out.”

“Tell me exactly what needs doing.”

Nikopo took a deep breath. Soon he explained that the planet’s death was inevitable. Scientists had tried to synthesise midenium, but failed. Perhaps they could have done it with a little more time… Or if so many crystals had not been used up in the war. Damn Lamens! His publications on midenium weapons had frightened me twenty years ago. Why had nobody realised where it would lead?… In any case, it was too late now. But the fact that so little energy remained explained why they needed me in particular.

That was then. Now three days had passed, and I was flying in a starship a little smaller than the Wing. There was only one seat, and even that was cramped. Sometimes I laugh when I remember his words about everything being planned “specifically for me”. At the time, grander, more heroic ideas had filled my head. It turned out he meant my diminutive size compared with the average Nebidenian’s two metres. Because I was small, I needed a small starship, and it needed less energy. That was all.

Of course, once I had handed over my weapon and gone with them to the government transport, they had fed me some more rubbish about my being one of the finest civilian pilots, whose considerable skill would be indispensable to the mission… But I knew the truth was simply a shortage of power. And it amused me enormously.

What else was there to do but laugh, when you knew your home planet had only a few days left? What else could you do when you realised you had seen the woman you loved for the last time? Seen Clara for the last time… The only one who was my match—in the sense that she was very small too. We had been born that way. We had loved each other so long, and this damned war had brought it to such a swift end…

The sphere of space moved very quickly across the glass of my new wings. Stars became thin lines, darting past the window and vanishing into darkness… It was a little chilly, but the starship kept out the cold, as did my two jumpers and jacket. Contact with command on Nebidenia had been lost five hours earlier. Now I flew in silence and solitude, left to spend the final hours of my life thinking about the past, the meaning of life and other nonsense.

Sometimes I thought how beautiful this world really was. I would fly past planets and watch hurricanes thousands of kilometres across slowly turning, and imagine what it would be like to find myself there. Some planets had active volcanoes that hurled lava straight into space. Some were tiny, others enormous… Multicoloured. Such a profusion of colours… Against black. Deep black, billions of light-years… And although everyone on Nebidenia had always tried to convince me I was small, in the infinity of space I felt large.

I knew that few were given the chance to be at the controls. To fly between worlds at such speed, deciding matters on the scale of life’s very existence in the system. Yet that was exactly what I was doing now. Perhaps none of it meant anything beside the immensity that had begun in nothingness; but the closer I came to my destination, the more certain I felt that I was not small.

So, here is what happened. Let me explain. For many years, the Nebidenians lived in peace and harmony, nourished by the crystals that grew everywhere on our green planet. We could skip breakfast if we were going to walk past the great midenium crystal in the city centre that afternoon. Simply passing it left you full and brimming with strength. That was how we lived.

Midenium would occasionally appear in random places around the planet. Later, scientists discovered it developed underground, like fungal spores, trying to join up its roots across the whole planet. I do not know why it needed to do that, and in truth I know less about natural science than I probably ought to. Anyway, sometimes its roots broke through the surface, and new crystals appeared.

About twenty-five years ago… A man called Richard Lamens published a book describing methods for turning the crystals into devices quite dangerous to life. Nevertheless, he became very popular and founded a company that made all sorts of useful things, from lasers for drilling wells to midenium kitchen knives that stayed sharp for ever.

He soon became a monopolist, then the richest man on the planet. About five years ago they found a tumour in his brain, and guess what he used to treat it? Crystals, of course. Lamens went quite mad about them. He began to imagine enemies everywhere and started manufacturing weapons—pistols, automatic rifles, artillery, missiles… Then organising private armies.

All of it was done in cooperation with the government of a neighbouring country, which attacked ours under Lamens’s leadership a few months ago. It was decided that anyone who refused to go to war and fight back would be executed. As you may have guessed, I became a deserter.

I ran away and hid, and I was afraid. Once more I was a little nobody… But how I longed, how I dreamed of living for ever. Of not dying. I tried not to think about the war, but to remember the happy times… If I had to die, let it be trying to kill the man who had started it all—trying to kill Richard Lamens. I had been preparing for that all this time. Unfortunately, I ran out of time. Nikopo took me to the base, where they explained everything, and I had neither time nor opportunity left to seek revenge.

So the Nebidenians only realised they had destroyed almost all the crystals when their weapons stopped working. By then it was too late…

They would not save themselves. Soon, very soon, the energy would disappear, and every Nebidenian would die without it. But the last hope was aboard my ship. The last hope lay within reach—a button which, pressed by my hand, could change the course of history.

The scientists had chosen the perfect candidate… The planet Odos, until recently a scorching ball of no use to anyone. Now it had begun to cool, producing condensation… And from that, an ocean. For the moment there was only one continent, a vast emptiness, but soon everything would change. Odos was already suitable for life. Perhaps one day it would arise by itself… But the chance was very small. Could it really happen again?…

The planet looked a little larger than Nebidenia. I was very close now… It was covered in dark blue, with a single gigantic black blot floating on it. The sensors triggered, and I activated the Last Shot protocol, which would target the right points near the coasts and launch three capsules at enormous speed, protected by a special metal alloy.

The capsules contained microorganisms. They had been specially modified from ordinary Nebidenian microbes to survive and develop on Odos. Most likely, they would first form colonies and settle on the seabed in shallow water. But all the conditions had been chosen so that, in time, they would evolve into something more… So that the planet would fill with oxygen through photosynthesis, which these little miracle-workers could already perform. So that it would become as green as our Nebidenia.

I pressed the button. The ship shuddered slightly, and a bright violet glow lit the windows. It quickly flared to white, blinding me, filling my whole head and striking the back of my skull. In that instant I vanished, carried into another time, or another world…

A vision came: I had been here before. Millions of years ago I, too, had hung in eternal space and thought about this. Thought about having been here before. And before that, and before that… The memories reached back to the beginning of time, though there had never been one. They twisted into a spiral, vanishing into the void. Yet the void was filled with colours no mind could imagine or see… It was everything and nothing at once. And I had been here since those very times, carrying out the same mission every few million years…

Back then, so long ago, I too had sent life to a lifeless planet. Perhaps that was how Nebidenia had come to be?… How thousands of planets before it had come to be, full of life doomed to perish… And doomed to return again when the time came. After I launched these capsules from the starship…

But the vision did not last. It turned into a dream in which nobody came for me on the day I met Clara. I climbed into the Wing and flew beyond the horizon, where I found Richard Lamens’s underground shelter. Breaking through the door was easy: I found a special opening where I placed an explosive device to good effect. Then I crawled through the ventilation shaft straight into his room… He sat at a desk, writing. Lamens no longer looked like the young genius he had seemed twenty-five years ago. He was fat and almost bald; weakness and disease were taking hold of his mind and body. I called to him. He turned. And the last thing he saw was my eyes, full of hatred and pain, full of the desire for vengeance for a ruined life and a slaughtered homeland. The pistol, with its tiny sliver of midenium, released an ultra-fast, concentrated blast of air that tore a hole in his body and took his life. For a moment I was satisfied. Then the dream ended.

Even awake, I was certain the dream was real. I had killed Richard Lamens, the man who created weapons and destroyed life. But now it seemed so trivial. There had already been thousands, millions, perhaps billions of Richard Lamenses… There had been so many deaths, so many births, that they seemed meaningless when you thought about it…

Then the moment passed, and the radiance slowly faded. The capsules were descending into the atmosphere of Odos, their shells burning away at such speed. I thought that I had sent shooting stars to Odos, though there was no one there yet to make a wish…

The thought brought me back to the scale of a small and insignificant life. But it still meant something to me. Perhaps I had already lived billions of years, but all I remembered were the last forty-five. Perhaps all these thoughts were simply the result of a massive discharge of midenium when the capsules were launched. Who knew? In any case, a little energy remained. I would turn the ship back towards Nebidenia, and perhaps the momentum of its dying engines and my faith in eternity would carry me home.

Perhaps some of us would survive and return to Odos when an ecosystem full of life and greenery had arisen here. Perhaps the people of Odos, conquering new lands, would return to Nebidenia, which would still have the potential for life. Or perhaps, before they died, they would all understand that they were alone in the universe. Understand that there had only ever been them, and would only ever be them—and then they would once again need a little man who could send a mysterious shower of stars to a young planet.

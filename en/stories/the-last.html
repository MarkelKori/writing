The Last

Markel Kori / 3 April 2020 / 4 min read

Before the humans came, this was Eden.

The gardens of paradise, or something like them. Perhaps even better… Once, nature flourished here. Birds the length of a bus flew overhead, half-tonne bees pollinated three-metre flowers, and the trees reached all the way to the heavens. Now and then, a little two-hundred-kilogram apple might fall on your head. But for some reason we decided to cross the line: instead of explorers, as we had intended, we became conquerors. And everything beautiful here began to disappear the moment a human foot touched the ground.

Sadly, simply by existing, humanity tends to treat its surroundings selfishly. Far to the north, and on the other planets in my system, you could feel it much more keenly. But here was the last place, the last refuge of peace.

The peace of paradise…

Sweet air, easy to breathe, needing neither masks nor filters—the last real air in this system. Air unspoiled by the gases pouring out of factories… A final chance to enjoy the green whisper of trees, reminding us of Mother Nature, whom we had so wretchedly betrayed and utterly destroyed.

My old friend Greg and I stood on the bank of a small river. Its slow current brushed against little stones, making a pleasant murmuring sound. When there had still been people living here besides Greg, we would sometimes get together and sing that old song, “Lazy River”. I loved Bobby Darin’s version.

Here and there, plants broke through the water—extraordinarily vivid and beautiful, perhaps as beautiful as those that had once grown on Earth, now reduced to a pitiful likeness of eternally burning Venus.

We were waiting for him. Gribbo: that was what we called the last of the Brobos. You could tell they were herbivores just by looking at them—small and furry, with great black eyes that pierced your soul… These little creatures spent most of their lives in the water or along its banks. They reminded Greg of penguins; I called them overgrown space hamsters. Unfortunately, people had seen nothing in them but exotic meat.

Every evening Gribbo swam downstream and stopped on the bank near our huts. He always came when the sky began turning pink and red. It was just that time now, and we were sitting by the water, waiting for him. Not to catch him and sell him as a curiosity. No. My old friend Greg and I were against that. We were the last guardians of the forests. Greg had spent his whole life in these tropics, protecting their pristine beauty; when I arrived on this planet, I met him and decided I might as well give the old man a hand.

And there he was, swimming towards us.

“Hey, look!” Greg finally interrupted my thoughts, behaving as though he had never seen a Brobos before.

Gribbo paddled his little paws slowly and clumsily, edging closer. I opened my hand to show him a few wild berries whose scent attracted the Brobos. He caught their smell and began moving faster. Then he was standing on the bank, up on his hind legs, sniffing my hand. Greg fed him some equally special fruit, and the creature wagged his tail happily before shuffling off into the undergrowth. We sat on a log nearby, and soon we heard strange sounds.

They seemed to rise from the depths, from the sea. There was something… unearthly about them. Something unreal. Their melancholy tone reminded me of a blue whale’s song, only sadder, perhaps.

“Gribbo’s calling for a mate again,” Greg said mournfully, holding back his tears. Before long they were slipping down his grey beard all the same. “It’s their spawning season. Gribbo wants to have young…”

I sighed heavily. Greg was catching the last rays of sunlight, perhaps remembering his youth.

“He still hopes there’s someone like him out there. Someone just like him,” Greg said.

Hope…

“Perhaps there is? Perhaps there are still Brobos nests on the Buitos Islands?” I asked.

“Even if there are, those islands don’t have long. The Confederation’s already planning to buy them up and build factories.”

“We’ll try to do something.”

“Of course, of course…” He gave a bitter laugh. “They’ll be here soon enough, too. We have to try, naturally, but I doubt there’s much to be done.”

I noticed something unusual in the sky…

Starships. Drawing inexorably closer.

“We’ll save the forest, Greg. I’m ready to give my life for it. The weapons are still where I left them, aren’t they?”

“Yes.”

“Then there’s nothing to worry about. We’ll hold out a little longer, even if no one answers our distress call. Gribbo will have at least one more night.”

This night.

The old man nodded.

And the lights in the distance grew brighter and brighter.

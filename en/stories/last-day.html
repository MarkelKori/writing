The Last Day

Markel Kori / 7 January 2025 / 5 min read

It was a warm evening in July. The sun was setting, spilling reds and violets across the sky. I was walking down a broad street, along the tree-lined path at its centre. There was no wind at all, and only a few scraps of candyfloss hung overhead.

I could hear the bell of a tram and the cries of gulls drifting up from the waterfront. The conversations of thousands of people floated around me, making the music of human company—something so familiar to me, a member of Homo sapiens.

I loved being surrounded by people, even strangers, even without saying a word. All that mattered was having someone nearby. That was why walking through the city centre always lifted my spirits, even in the worst of times.

The people around me were lightly and beautifully dressed: girls in colourful, patterned dresses, young men in summer shirts and shorts. Some held hands, some embraced, some sat laughing on benches. Everyone seemed happy on this lovely summer day.

All of them would go on living on this planet… Except me.

This was my last day on planet Earth. My last chance to see green trees, to hear the rustle of leaves, to catch the perfume of girls passing by… The last time I would go down into the metro, the last time I would study the clouds.

It was, of course, an important day for me. Many people would probably have thrown a tremendous party, got drunk, hired a prostitute. None of it suited me. Drinking, at least, was strictly forbidden. The rest I ruled out because of the sort of person I was…

There was no one I wanted to share this last day with. There were distant acquaintances, friends I sometimes met to pass the time in utterly pointless ways… None of them was close enough to be with me in my final hours on Earth.

What truly felt like family were the streets of this city and the strangers who sometimes offered me a friendly smile. I walked past all the places that had mattered in my life, remembering how I had spent the last twenty-eight years.

I passed the steps where I had sat with the girl I loved fourteen years ago… My first love, which had fallen apart almost as soon as it began. My heart was broken, and I came back to sit here more than once, trying to make sense of everything that had happened.

Years passed. I healed, and met someone else. Another city, another time… The love was not as bright or as fierce as it had been the first time. But it was love! It lit a little flame in my soul again, and I will always remember a kiss among the trees of Berlin’s Tiergarten… Her ardent lips, saying, “If only this night would never end!”

But it all passed as quickly as it had begun. I was left with nothing, and soon fate began taking away everyone close to me, using its most terrible instrument—death…

For years I was broken, with no idea what to do with the rest of my life. Neither work nor my old hobbies brought me any pleasure. Life seemed grey and entirely without meaning.

Then one day I was sitting at my computer when I saw a news item: “Crew recruitment opens for first mission to the Red Planet”…

However badly life had damaged my mind, my body remained in excellent shape. I had always had good marks in PE, and I could eat as much as I liked without ever putting on weight. It took very little exercise for me to build muscle. Genetics had simply given me that gift. Nor had I ever been seasick or airsick. As a child I could spin on a roundabout for as long as I wanted without getting dizzy.

I was suitable. And they chose me.

Despite all the wonders of science we had created in recent years, returning home was “technically impossible”. But that did not stop me.

And tomorrow, I would hear the countdown…

Three!… And images of the past will flash through my mind again. The women who left me, taking my heart with them… My family, who are gone… My city, which has changed so much over the years…

Two!… And I remember myself as a little boy in my mother’s arms. Her warmth, her kind smile, her tender care for me… And it seems to me that this is my most important memory.

One! Liftoff!…

And the fire flares up that will carry me towards the stars. I have always looked at the night sky in wonder at the vastness and mystery of our world. So many secrets hidden out there in the darkness of space… And everything here on Earth so ordinary, so easily understood.

Here, everyone thinks only of themselves and their little troubles. No one cares about anyone else. No one thinks about what they could do for this small planet. All the wars over land, all the quarrels over money, all the tyrants and leaders, all the love and hatred—it all stays here, on Earth. On this tiny ball amid the immensity of space.

And when you look at other worlds, when you understand how rich our universe is, all earthly troubles seem trivial. Every person feels this sometimes… Then sleeps, wakes up, and forgets. And the evil that is slowly killing us all begins again…

I believe the world can be better. That we can live without war, that we can live longer and happier lives.

Hopes are like waves: they travel a long way towards the shore, then dash themselves against the rocks of reality. But that does not sadden me. My hope cannot be destroyed! Each time, it comes alive again and grows stronger than before.

For me, the flight to Mars is another step of hope and faith. I believe in the triumph of science, in the fulfilment of our dreams of breaking through barriers and reaching out into space.

And now, as the rocket rises and leaves everything earthly behind… I look back at my little blue ball with a smile, and feel love for every creature living on it.
